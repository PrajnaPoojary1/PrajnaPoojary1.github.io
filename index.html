02 / LEADERSHIP

My Leadership Philosophy

People First

I believe people are the foundation of every business objective. Before asking how an organization will achieve its goals, leaders should ask whether they have the right capabilities and the right people to achieve them—today and in the future. That means understanding the skills the business needs, hiring thoughtfully, developing people continuously and creating an environment where employees can do their best work. When we create the conditions for people to thrive, they are better positioned to create value for customers and the business.

Future Ready

The pace of technological and organizational change means leaders cannot focus only on what the business needs today. With technologies such as AI reshaping how organizations work, I continually ask whether the workforce is developing the capabilities the business will need in the future, and whether technology, systems and processes are evolving alongside them. The goal is to prepare early enough that when tomorrow becomes today, the organization is positioned to thrive rather than simply react.

Ownership

I believe ownership is one of the most important qualities in both engineers and leaders. Ownership means taking accountability for the outcome—not simply completing an assigned task. It means understanding the objective, anticipating risks, communicating early when challenges arise and doing what is necessary to deliver with quality and within the agreed timeline. Strong ownership creates trust, improves execution and gives teams confidence that commitments will be followed through.

Continuous Learning

Change is the only constant. In a rapidly evolving ecosystem, being prepared and skilled for yesterday’s needs does not necessarily make us relevant to today’s challenges—this is true for engineers, leaders and organizations alike. I believe leaders have an important role in creating an ecosystem that feeds on continuous learning, where people are encouraged to adapt, develop new capabilities and remain curious. If an organization wants to maintain a competitive edge, it must stay relevant not only today, but also tomorrow. That is only possible when continuous learning becomes part of the culture, supported and reinforced by management and leaders at every level.

Collaboration & Shared Success

Complex business problems rarely belong to one function. I believe collaboration becomes more powerful when people from different backgrounds, disciplines and experiences come together, because diverse perspectives can reveal ideas and approaches that may otherwise be overlooked. People can see the same problem from very different angles, creating opportunities for more thoughtful and innovative solutions. Collaboration also creates a sense of togetherness—bringing people around a shared objective, building shared ownership and creating the collective commitment needed for success.

05 / RESEARCH

Exploring the future of work and skills.

Workforce Upskilling in the Indian IT Sector for AI and Automation

MSc Dissertation · University of Bath · 2025

This qualitative, interpretivist study examined the workforce upskilling methods most adopted by Indian IT organisations in response to AI and automation, addressing a gap in prior research that identified many approaches but offered less clarity on which methods organisations prioritize in practice.

The research found that on-the-job learning was the most adopted approach, followed closely by e-learning platforms such as LinkedIn Learning and Udemy. Employees and managers consistently highlighted these approaches as practical, scalable and immediately applicable to workplace needs.

The study highlighted a dual perspective on effective upskilling: employees seek personalised, flexible and practical learning that fits their individual needs and project realities, while HR and managers emphasise strategic prioritisation, business alignment, selective investment and return on investment. The central recommendation is to make upskilling more purposeful and context-specific—aligning learning opportunities with actual project requirements and individual needs while strategically directing investment toward business-critical skills and capability gaps.

Participants9 interviews: 6 employees, 2 managers, 1 HR professional
MethodQualitative interpretivist research + thematic analysis
Core findingOn-the-job learning followed by e-learning platforms
Key recommendationMake upskilling personalised, practical and flexible while strategically aligning learning investment with business-critical skills and capability needs
